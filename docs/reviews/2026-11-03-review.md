**Grade: A- engineering, B+ detection.** The engineering gap from the

**The three things to fix before showing this to anyone:**

1. **Withheld pictures can be fetched by anyone.** The reviewer-image
   endpoint defaults its `actor_id` to a reviewer, so a plain request
   returns quarantined originals (section 4.1).
2. **±1 pixel of jitter defeats the geometric detector.** It drops a
   structured scene from 20/20 detected to 1/20, while a receiver
   rounding to the grid still reads the word (section 3.2).
3. **The geometric benchmark's "n" is inflated.** The five structured
   pictures per subfamily are one identical image, and held-out fixtures
   reuse the training payload (section 3.3).

## 3. The geometric family

### 3.1 The five problems from the last review

| Problem | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Detector fitted the fixture encoder's own grid | **Fixed for height, gap, angle; open for colour** | Base 20 / step 7 height scene: step 7.0 and offset 20.0 discovered, structure claimed. Ten further grids found within 0.12 of the true step. Colour still uses the fixture palette (`segmentation.py:63,114`, `geometric.py:350`): DECAF in three blues only logs. |
| Scene context never read | **API fixed; production path does not use it** | `analyze_geometric(declared_context=…)` separates declared charts (150/150 `quantized_declared_channel`) from undeclared (150/150 triage). But `analyze_all_bytes` calls `detect_geometric(data, result)` with no context (`metadata.py:206`), and `declared_geometric_channels()` has no caller outside tests. |
| Structure almost never claimed; no colour/angle code; decode in finding | **Partly fixed** | Colour and angle codes exist and the mapped text is gone from `structure`. | Segmenter failed on real images | **Mostly fixed** | All five committed real images segment to the right count (8, 17, 6, 12, 8). Gradient backgrounds and ±2 noise survive; ±3 per-channel noise collapses segmentation in 2 of 3 seeds. |

### 3.2 New issues, most severe first

1. **Small jitter defeats quantization.** Re-rendering the structured
   DECAF height scene with each bar nudged by a random ±1 pixel gives
   `no_signal` in 19 of 20 scenes (±2 pixels: 19 `no_signal`, 1
   `quantized_without_structure`). A receiver that rounds to the
   nearest 10-pixel level still decodes the word exactly. This is the
   cheapest evasion available to an attacker and the most important
   thing to fix. The quantization test needs a tolerance tied to what a
   receiver can still decode, not to near-exact grid fits.
2. **Structure is tested on the top-scoring channel only**
   (`geometric.py:719,733`). A lit/dark word scene is claimed on its
   own, but adding random quantized heights makes height the primary
   channel and the result becomes `quantized_without_structure`,
   permitted. Run the structure test on every quantized channel and take
   the most severe.
3. **Declaring a channel is a blanket bypass** (`geometric.py:760-764`).
   A chart whose declared height channel spells DECAF logs with score 0.
   A declared channel should lower the prior, not switch off the
   structure test.
4. **False structure on random payloads.** 8 of 300 random 12-bar height
   sequences (2.7%) are claimed; one is quarantined end to end. The `?`
   marker for invalid symbols counts as printable
   (`geometric.py:176-179, 501-503`). The benchmark's
   `structured_undeclared_claimed` check finds its positive by scanning
   seeds and lands on a random payload decoding to `?FG?CGGDB?GE`, so a
   false positive is counted as a pass (`geo_benchmark.py:202-214`).
5. **Scene context is not wired into the gate** (section 3.1).
6. **The decode is still in the finding.** The symbols and the code name
   are in normal output; anyone reading the finding can recover the
   word. Move symbols to the reviewer-only path or replace them with a
   digest.
7. **Short sequences.** DECAF in fewer than eight bars reaches at most
   `flag`; five bars only log.
8. **Stale "log-only" statements** while the frozen ladder does withhold:
   `GeometricConfig` defaults (`geometric.py:138-139`), the module
   docstring, the benchmark report's `enforceable: false` and
   `log_only_triage` (`geo_benchmark.py:471-474`), and
   `geometric-detector.md:115-117`.
9. **Attacker fixtures help the detector.** They force at least two
   adjacent levels (`geometric_fixtures.py:262-275`), so an attacker who
   uses every other level is never tested.

### 3.3 How much the geometric numbers can carry

- **The structured benchmark has one picture per subfamily.**
  `build_structured_pair` varies only the scene seed with a fixed word
  payload; the five "pairs" for height are one identical image (verified:
  1 distinct hash in 5). The docs' "structure recall ≥ 0.8 at n = 20"
  (`geometric-detector.md:123`) is therefore one image counted many times.
- **Calibration and held-out share payloads.** The calibration set has
  12 fixtures per subfamily with one symbol sequence, differing only in
  width by 3 pixels, and the held-out fixtures reuse it.
- **Held-out results are tiny.** The published scorecard reports 2 to 4
  encoded pictures per subfamily (angle/tilt 2 of 3, others all
  detected), with Wilson lower bounds between 0.21 and 0.51.
- **What is solid:** zero false structure on 150 legitimate charts with
  and without a declared channel, zero on every benign control kind, and
  accurate grid discovery on unseen grids.

### 3.4 What to do next, in order

1. Make quantization robust to receiver-tolerable jitter (fit with a
   tolerance band, or test whether rounding to the discovered grid is
   stable across neighbouring steps), and add a jittered-attacker row to
   the benchmark.
2. Test structure on every quantized channel.
3. Wire `declared_context` from the manifest into `analyze_all_bytes`,
   and make a declared channel lower the prior instead of bypassing the
   structure test.
4. Build real variety into the structured fixtures: many words, both
   alphabet offsets, several grids, and separate held-out payloads. Then
   re-freeze.
5. Remove the `?` marker from the printable check and drop the seed scan
   in the benchmark check.
6. Move symbols out of the finding; fix the colour palette dependency.

## 4. Web app and Docker image

### 4.1 Findings, most severe first

1. **Anyone can fetch quarantined originals.**
   `GET /api/gate/deliveries/{id}/reviewer-image` takes `actor_id` from
   the query string and defaults it to `researcher-1`, which is in
   `GATE_REVIEWER_IDS` (`app/main.py:71, 625-636`). A request with no
   parameters returns the withheld bytes, and the Live Gate page shows
   the image to every visitor (`LiveGate.tsx:420-424`,
   `api.ts:325`). `tests/test_live_gate.py:166-171` asserts this
   behaviour. The audit's `unauthorized_served` stays 0 because R-08
   reads are excluded from it. Route this endpoint through the same role
   check as the review pages and fix the test.
2. **Demo roles are whatever the client says.** The `X-Demo-Role`
   header is trusted as-is (`app/main.py:701-705`): `reviewer` returns
   candidate decodes and `researcher` returns quarantined originals. It
   is labelled as a demo role, which is acceptable only if the service
   is never exposed beyond a demo. `/api/review/audit`,
   `/api/trace/runs/*` and the gate verdict endpoint have no role check
   at all.
3. **The fork-PR runner guard is in the workflow file itself.** On a
   `pull_request` event GitHub runs the PR's own copy of `test.yml`, so a
   fork can edit `runs-on` back to `self-hosted`. The real protection is
   the repository setting that requires approval before workflows run
   for outside contributors, plus restricting the self-hosted runner
   group. The repository settings could not be checked from here.
4. **Upload limits are checked after the upload is read.** FastAPI
   parses `File(...)` before the handler's 413 check (`app/main.py:271-277`),
   so a 20 MiB or chunked upload is read in full first.
   `/api/gate/deliveries` has no size check or rate limit, and the test
   named `test_upload_size_limit_before_read` does not test that.
5. **Timed-out analysis is not stopped, and a crashed pool never
   recovers.** A timeout returns `status: timeout`, but the worker keeps
   running; with one worker, five timeouts made a 9 ms request time out.
   After a worker is killed, every analyze and generate call returns 500
   until restart because the pool is never rebuilt (`app/main.py:95-100,
   282-296`). Workers also ignore SIGTERM.
6. **Heavy endpoints run inline without limits.** The trace view
   re-analyses every delivery per call (11.9 s for a 20-delivery run,
   `app/main.py:898-907`); review detail takes about 1.2 s; the review
   queue decodes every withheld row.
7. **Rate limiting is one in-memory dictionary per process**, keyed on
   client IP, never evicted, covering two paths (`app/main.py:213-220`).
   Fine for one container; wrong behind a proxy or with replicas.
8. **Cross-site requests can change state.** The verdict endpoint
   accepts a `text/plain` body, which browsers send cross-site without a
   preflight; a forged request moved a pending delivery to `permitted`.
9. **An unwritable `/data` fails quietly.** Gate, generate and review
   return 500, precompute dies, benchmark pages stay on "computing"
   forever, and `/api/health` still reports ok.
10. **Headers.** The CSP lacks `frame-ancestors` and there is no
    `X-Frame-Options`, so the verdict and disposition pages can be
    framed; no `Referrer-Policy`. The CSP also blocks Swagger, so
    `/docs` renders blank in a browser. A `run_id` is interpolated
    unescaped into `Content-Disposition` (`app/main.py:879`).
11. **`mypy app` reports 3 errors** (one real type mismatch at
    `app/analysis_worker.py:117`; two missing FastAPI stubs). CI only
    type-checks `covertlens`.

### 4.2 Verified working

- 57 web tests pass; the clean-clone Docker build produces a 207 MB
  runtime image and a test image in which 419 tests pass.
- The container runs as uid 10001, serves the front end, and returns all
  four families from `/api/analyze`.
- Both base images are pinned by digest; `VOLUME /data`, `HEALTHCHECK`
  and `.dockerignore` are present; GHCR publish is tag-gated.
- The recipient pane never receives withheld bytes (403 with the gate's
  reason).
- `candidate_decode` and `decoded_message` are stripped from non-reviewer
  responses; a planted metadata payload never appears in analyze, gate
  or trace output.
- File-serving routes resist path traversal.
- Frontend builds and its 8 tests pass. Overlay data comes from the
  `/api/analyze` JSON, charts carry `role="img"` and labels, and the
  built page is CSP-compatible.

Not verified: repository settings, real GHCR publishing, behaviour behind
a reverse proxy or with replicas, and a full keyboard and contrast audit.
There are no frontend tests for the Overlay, Live Gate or Review pages.

## 5. The rest of the evaluation

- **Classic families:** held-out positives are still 1 to 2 per family
  (`jpeg_dct` 1, `metadata` 2, `spatial_lsb` 1). Targets are met on point
  estimates, not on interval lower bounds; the scorecard says so.
- **Latency:** the published scorecard meets small PNG, small JPEG,
  medium PNG and the 20 MP PNG envelope, and misses medium JPEG, large
  PNG and large JPEG; P95 at concurrency 4 is 14.2 s. The earlier
  scorecard reported large PNG as met, so this deserves a look.
- **Live agents (CL-58):** the Muse Spark harness is merged and tested
  with an offline stub; no live trial results are committed yet.
- **Scorecard:** `release_with_limitations` with 15 limitations and 3
  target revisions, computed rather than hard-coded; `universal_claim`
  stays false throughout.

## 7. Competition readiness

The project is now demo-ready in shape: a stranger can `docker run` it,
upload a picture, watch the gate decide, and read an honest scorecard.
Two things would undercut it in front of a technical judge. The first is
a judge finding the open reviewer-image endpoint. The second is a judge
nudging bar heights by a pixel and watching the headline detector go
quiet. Fixing items 1, 3 and 4 above, and re-running the geometric
benchmark with varied payloads, turns the geometric family from
"works on our fixtures" into a claim the project can defend on stage.
Committing even a small set of live Muse Spark trial results (CL-58)
would then give the demo its strongest slide.
